Thin then Thick Description 

You need to systematically do the groundwork of carefully describing what the works are - i.e. what are they made of, what do they look like, etc. Begin with what anthropologist Clifford Geertz called 'thin description'. Thin description refers to a straightforward, surface-level account of a particular cultural practice without delving into its deeper meanings or context. It provides basic facts, often in a straightforward and objective manner. Thin descriptions may lack the broader context and cultural significance that would give the practice deeper meaning. Only having provided some form of thin description can you move into thick description, providing a detailed, nuanced account of the work by exploring its context, underlying meanings, and the social dynamics at play.
